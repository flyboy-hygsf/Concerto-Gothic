The Context

Concerto Gothic began with a single page of handwritten musical score annotations. These annotations were shorthand that only Gordon Parks could read. Slashes for beat counts, numbers for phrasing. This was a clear example of a unique creative language that belonged entirely to his compositional process. It was Instinctual, gestural.

The alphabet at the core of this experience, was built by extracting the structural DNA of those annotations and translating them into a system of letterforms through the practice of atypography. Every letter is constructed from vertical bars of varying heights and widths that make the shapes and widths of the lines in the original notes.

The final layer of translation transforms the visual alphabet into sound. Each of the 26 letters is mapped to a specific musical note so that any word typed by a user generates a unique melodic phrase. The notes were chosen using a C minor pentatonic scale with a Dorian second, a six-note palette foundational to jazz, blues, gospel, and West African musical traditions. Every name and every word produces a melody that feels intentionally composed even though it's generated from the act of spelling.

The result is a three-stage translation. Handwritten annotations become a visual alphabet. The visual alphabet becomes a sonic system. And the user's input becomes a composition.
